At a glance

★ = world-famous. These are the sights people back home will recognise. Look for the star in each day's See & do list.

Famous, but left out (too far for this route)

  • Preikestolen (Pulpit Rock) and Trolltunga: each needs a full extra day from Bergen/Stavanger.
  • Geirangerfjord / Trollstigen (needs a long drive) and northern lights in Tromsø (needs a northern flight).
  • LEGO House (Billund): 3+ h each way from Copenhagen. Use the LEGO Store on Strøget instead.
  • Gothenburg / Malmö in Sweden and Stockholm's Gripsholm Castle: not on the path of this route.
  • World-famous brands, Sweden. Too far or no public visit: Volvo (Volvo Museum, Gothenburg), Saab (Saab Car Museum, Trollhättan), Absolut Vodka (distillery in Åhus, far south), Oatly and Tetra Pak (Malmö and Lund), plus Spotify, Ericsson and Electrolux (Stockholm HQs, no public tour). Easy shop stops: H&M flagships on Drottninggatan and Hamngatan, Acne Studios and Fjällräven stores in central Stockholm.
  • World-famous brands, Norway. Too far: Hurtigruten (the Bergen → Kirkenes coastal voyage, 6+ days), Equinor (Stavanger HQ, no tour). Easy shop stops: Helly Hansen and Norrøna outdoor wear (Oslo and Bergen stores), Dale of Norway knitwear (factory in Dale, about 30 min by train from Bergen), Freia chocolate and Jarlsberg cheese from any supermarket.
  • World-famous brands, Denmark. Too far: LEGO House (Billund, 3+ h each way). No public tour: Maersk and Novo Nordisk HQs. Easy shop stops, central Copenhagen (Strøget and Østergade): Royal Copenhagen porcelain, Georg Jensen silver, Bang & Olufsen, Pandora jewellery, Flying Tiger Copenhagen, ECCO shoes and Joe & the Juice. Take home the blue tin of Royal Dansk butter cookies and Lurpak butter.
  • World-famous brands, Iceland. Easy shop stops in Reykjavik: 66°North outerwear (Laugavegur), the Handknitting Association of Iceland for lopapeysa sweaters (Skólavörðustígur), Omnom chocolate (factory shop and tour at the Grandi harbour) and the pink-pig Bónus supermarket. Detour: Reyka vodka distillery in Borgarnes, a good stop on the Snæfellsnes drive on Day 23.
  • Already covered: IKEA (Älmhult), LEGO (Store on Strøget, Day 19), Carlsberg (Valby), ABBA, Nobel and the Blue Lagoon.

Check these before booking

  • Oslo Viking Ship Museum (Bygdøy) is closed while it is rebuilt as the Museum of the Viking Age. The reopening was expected around 2027, so confirm on khm.uio.no. Day 8 has a Viking fallback.
  • Seasonal openings: Carl Larsson-gården (Sundborn), the Sognefjord express boat and Skansen/Gröna Lund hours vary or reduce in Nov–Apr. Tivoli runs Halloween and Christmas editions in autumn and is closed for some weeks in winter.
  • Iceland: the Reykjanes peninsula (Blue Lagoon, Grindavík) has had repeated volcanic eruptions since 2021, and the Blue Lagoon has closed temporarily. Check bluelagoon.com and safetravel.is before Day 24 and keep Sky Lagoon as a backup. Dyrhólaey is usually closed for bird nesting from about May to mid-June.
  • Daylight: in Nov days are about 6–7 h long, so keep outdoor sights for the morning. Dec–Mar has snow; Apr–May is mild but fjord services are still ramping up.

Self-drive essentials (Apr–May)

  • Licence: carry your Indian driving licence plus an International Driving Permit. Rental companies commonly ask for it, so confirm with yours. Drive on the right.
  • Insurance: add gravel (GP) and sand and ash (SAAP) cover. Wind damage to car doors is common and is usually not covered, so park facing the wind and hold the door with both hands.
  • Check daily: road.is (road conditions), vedur.is (weather and wind) and safetravel.is. Highland F-roads are closed in spring, and off-road driving is illegal. A 2WD is fine on the routes below.
  • Daylight: about 14 h in mid-April and 18–20 h by late May, so long driving days work. Headlights stay on at all times. Fuel up when the tank is half empty.
  • Pools: everyone showers with soap, without a swimsuit, before entering any pool or lagoon. It is strictly enforced.
  • Northern Lights end around mid-April (nights get too bright). Puffins arrive from about mid-April, with tours from Reykjavik in May.
  • Money: Iceland is one of Europe's most expensive countries. Cards work everywhere, tap water is excellent, and supermarkets (Bónus, Krónan) help for packed lunches.

Essentials

Getting there and away (Mumbai)

  • There is no nonstop flight from Mumbai to Stockholm. Common one-stop routes are via Helsinki, Doha, Dubai, Istanbul or a European hub.
  • Fly into Stockholm (ARN) and out of Keflavik (KEF), Reykjavik as an open-jaw ticket, with the Copenhagen → Reykjavik hop in between. There is no nonstop to Mumbai, so connect via a European hub.
  • A single Schengen visa covers all four countries (Sweden, Norway, Denmark and Iceland are all in the Schengen area). Apply to the country where you spend the most nights, which is Sweden or Norway here. Check requirements for your passport.

Money and getting around

  • Cards and Apple/Google Pay are accepted nearly everywhere. Carry very little cash.
  • City transport apps: SL (Stockholm), Ruter (Oslo), Skyss (Bergen), DOT / Rejsekort (Copenhagen).
  • Book in advance: Stockholm–Oslo train (SJ), Oslo–Myrdal (Vy), Flåmsbana, the Nærøyfjord cruise, the Bergen flight and the cruise cabin.
  • Tap water is excellent everywhere. Bring a refillable bottle.

Must-try food, quick list

  • Sweden: köttbullar (meatballs with lingonberry), kanelbulle and kardemummabulle at fika, falukorv, Västerbotten cheese.
  • Norway: fiskesuppe (fish soup), brunost (brown cheese on waffles), kjøttkaker, skillingsboller, fjord trout.
  • Iceland: kjötsúpa (lamb soup), skyr, plokkfiskur (fish stew), the Icelandic hot dog (pylsur), geothermal rye bread, and for the brave, hákarl (fermented shark). Reykjavik has Indian restaurants such as Austur-Indíafjelagið (check it is open).
  • Denmark: smørrebrød, wienerbrød (Danish pastry), pølser (hot dogs), flødeboller, frikadeller.
